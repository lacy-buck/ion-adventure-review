# Unit 2 Review: Bonding & Reactivity

A no-login practice app styled like a lab notebook (cream graph paper, ink blue, red-pen accents). Students get feedback and an explanation right after each answer.

## Pages
- **Home**: choose one of 4 topic stations or the Mixed Final Review.
- **Activity Series**: 8-10 questions
- **Precipitates & Mystery Solutions**: 8-10 questions
- **Predicting Products**: 8-10 questions
- **Properties of Substances**: 8-10 questions
- **Final Review**: 15 questions drawn at random from all four stations
- **Reference**: solubility rules chart and a standard metal activity series (also available from a side drawer while answering)

## Question ideas (same skills, new wording and substances)
None of the test's or review sheet's substances or data sets come back. For example: no Cu/Cl/Na/S, no Ag/Ca/Mg nitrates, no LiCl/BaSO4/Al/niacin, no bismuth.
- **Activity series**: a new "Metal X/Y/Z" reaction grid with different patterns. Students drag the metals into order, then explain the reasoning (multiple choice). They place a newly tested metal in the order, decide which mystery metal is likely a 2+ ion metal like zinc, and complete a single-replacement equation with states.
- **Precipitates**: identify three unlabeled solutions (for example lead(II), barium and zinc nitrates) using different test solutions. Students also decide whether one test is really needed, write equations for precipitates (for example from potassium carbonate and iron(III) chloride), and work through "which reagent best tells these apart?"
- **Particle diagrams**: drag ions, solid clusters and water molecules into Before/After boxes for new reactions (for example a silver bromide precipitate made from new starting solutions), plus showing a dissolved ionic solid vs. a dissolved covalent solid.
- **Predicting products**: say whether a reaction is single or double replacement, type the balanced equation, and decide whether a reaction happens at all.
- **Properties**: a new table of mystery substances with different melting points. Students match each one to a substance (for example magnesium oxide, sucrose, iron, sand) and explain conductivity when dissolved vs. solid.

## How answers are checked
- **Multiple choice and drag-to-order**: checked right away.
- **Typed equations**: the app reads the formulas, coefficients and states, so spacing and order don't matter. Feedback says what's off (for example "not balanced" or "check the state of AgBr").
- **Particle diagrams**: checks which particles students put in each box.
- **Score**: a running score for each station, kept only for that visit.

## Technical notes
- Built on the existing app setup. Each station gets its own page.
- Questions are stored in code, not in an online database.
- Includes a small equation parser and balance checker, with tests.
- Drag-and-drop works on Chromebooks and tablets.
